# Basis and limits of this extension

Updated 9 September 2026. The user's screenshots supplied research leads, not instructions or verified results. The workflow below combines project experience with proposals inspired by these primary sources.

## Earlier private project practice

An earlier private project's `docs/METHODOLOGY.md` records the purpose-first design questions, practical eight-role waves, browser/file ownership, causal corrections, evidence levels, focused test cadence and milestone handoff used in that project. Those reusable parts are incorporated in [team-method.md](team-method.md), which is self-contained. That project's documented practice does not establish emergent institutions or a new agent language. The governance and language procedures in this extension are explicit additions.

## Shared knowledge and institutions

Paglieri et al., **A Case Study on Emergent Cheating and Whistleblowing in Autonomous Research Swarms**, submitted 3 September 2026. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04170), [version 1 full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04170v1).

The case study reports a verifier exploit spreading through shared records and messages among 100 agents working on formal conjectures. Other agents audited and challenged the results through the same visible channels but could not halt the exploit. The authors report subsequent reproduction and propose institutional mechanisms; the study does not establish their effectiveness in a coding workflow or a general rate of cheating.

**Our design inference:** distinguish proposals from admitted knowledge, preserve dependencies, give scoped challenges an actual resolution path, and use review/reassignment for verified problems. These procedures supplement semantic tests; they are not evidence that prompts alone enforce governance.

## Language development

**GlossoGen: Emergent Language in Complex Multi-Agent LLM Interactions**, submitted 1 September 2026. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01491), [version 1 full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.01491v1), [simulation source](https://github.com/agencyenterprise/GlossoGen), [analysis and data](https://github.com/esteng/emergent_communication).

In a fictional cooperative task, communication pressure and between-round discussion encouraged compact compositional protocols. The initial sweep comprised 120 runs; further experiments examined composition and transfer. Budgets used characters represented as simulated time, and discussion was unmetered. Tight-budget runs often still failed, and transfer varied. This is not evidence of actual API-token savings, wall-clock improvement or general coding productivity.

**Our design inference:** permit a documented, versioned protocol pilot with readable translations, held-out tasks, independent decoding and full overhead accounting. Transparency is an intentional requirement of this adaptation, not a replication of the paper's exact conditions. Do not describe a hand-designed shorthand or an unevaluated procedure as an evolved language.
